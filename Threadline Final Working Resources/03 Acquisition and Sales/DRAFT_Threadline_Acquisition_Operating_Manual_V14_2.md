# Threadline Acquisition Operating Manual - 90 Day V14.2 Course-Authority

## Mission
Create enough qualified buyer conversations to reach at least two retained GBP 2.5k/month clients inside 90 days, using a measurable acquisition system rather than channel preference or motivation.

## Course-derived acquisition architecture
Treat acquisition as seven components: **scheduler, platform, stimulus, CRM, lead source, metric tracker, delivery mechanism**. The strategy is the whole system. A platform is only one component.

## 0. Prerequisite - Market Validation / Niche Gate
Before scaled outbound, execute SOP 00 V14. One active niche hypothesis only. Immersion comes first, then direct problem/solution validation. Complete the first five research conversations and confirm a recurring expensive core pain strongly enough to test commercially. The mentor's 4-of-5 rule is a useful operating heuristic; the course's Problem/Solution Validation principle governs. The first list, message and channel must come from the market's economics, language, validated pain and reachability.

## 1. Reverse-engineer the 90-day goal
Formula:
`first touches required = target wins / (booking rate x show rate x qualified rate x close rate)`

Divide by remaining acquisition workdays. Start with temporary assumptions only until actual data exists. Example: 2 wins x 2% booking x 75% show x 75% qualified x 15% close implies about 1,185 first touches across 60 workdays, around 20/day.

Every Friday freeze raw counts first, then replace assumptions with actuals where the sample is meaningful and recalculate. Keep early conservative assumptions visible beside tiny-sample actuals so one outlier does not whipsaw the plan. The quota is a **math output**, not a motivational slogan.

Never import another creator's example rates as Threadline benchmarks. Their numbers can illustrate the chain; only Threadline data can set Threadline's operating quota.

## 2. Launch channel hypothesis
### Primary outbound delivery - cold email
Use qualified B2B cold email as the first controlled delivery hypothesis because it is direct, measurable and closest to the source acquisition architecture. Keep compliance/relevance standards high.

### LinkedIn - support layer
Use LinkedIn to source, inspect, contextualise, build credibility and support follow-up. Do not run an unrelated second LinkedIn machine just to feel busy.

### A-fit personalised Loom
Use 1-2 short diagnoses/Looms for exceptional accounts where the potential economics justify extra effort.

### Warm/referral
Track separately because conversion characteristics differ from cold.

### Inbound/content
Own content educates the market, increases trust and can later create appointments. It does not replace the required outbound reps until data shows it reliably does so.

## 3. Lead gate
Do not contact a company merely to hit volume. Most must be true:
- active niche hypothesis fit;
- proven offer / economics support Threadline;
- expertise materially influences trust/purchase;
- content/personal-brand opportunity exists;
- capacity for demand;
- reachable buyer;
- visible symptom or reason for relevance.

Classify A/B/C. Do not contact C.

## 4. Daily operating sequence
1. Open scoreboard and current funnel math.
2. Handle live replies and overdue follow-ups first.
3. Source enough A/B-fit accounts for today's calculated first-touch requirement.
4. Verify business, buyer, economics, content symptom and one specific observation.
5. Send the current versioned stimulus via the current primary delivery mechanism.
6. Use LinkedIn context/support where useful.
7. Create A-fit Looms selectively.
8. Book qualified diagnosis calls.
9. Log every touch/reply/outcome/message version/source/hours.
10. Only after revenue actions are complete, work on own content.

## 5. Stimulus principles
- Observation/problem-led, not generic agency pitch.
- Show enough understanding to earn attention, not a fake diagnosis from three minutes of browsing.
- Give a simple next step.
- Avoid manufactured urgency, fake scarcity, deceptive identities or spam.
- Version the message and preserve learning.

## 6. Follow-up
Use respectful multi-touch follow-up over roughly 2-3 weeks. Add useful context rather than repeated "just bumping this". Stop immediately on no/opt-out.

## 7. Loom/diagnosis structure
1. Why this account caught attention.
2. One business/content observation.
3. Careful constraint hypothesis + evidence.
4. 2-3 tests/angles.
5. One relevant Threadline loop/screen if useful.
6. Clear CTA to diagnose properly.

## 8. Booking and sales handoff
The acquisition system ends in a qualified diagnosis call. Booking form should collect only what improves the call. Every booked call moves to SOP 03.

## 9. Daily metrics
Track: qualified prospects, first touches, follow-ups, replies, positive replies, bookings, shows, qualified calls, offers, wins, cash, hours, source/channel, message version, direct cost.

Calculate: booking rate, show rate, qualified rate, close rate, cash/100 touches, cash/qualified call, acquisition hours/client, practical CAC.

## 10. Friday acquisition-control review
1. Freeze the week's raw counts.
2. Recalculate booking, show, qualification and close rates.
3. Recalculate remaining first touches and next week's daily quota.
4. Find the first broken conversion: reach/contact -> reply -> positive -> booking -> show -> qualified -> offer -> close -> cash -> delivery quality.
5. Change one meaningful variable aimed at that constraint.
6. Preserve the previous message/channel/version for comparison.
7. Review warm/referral, cold outbound and inbound separately when volume permits.

If the required daily volume becomes absurdly high, do not merely brute-force it. Diagnose whether the niche, reachability, message, show rate, qualification, close rate, price/proof or offer economics are making the math bad.

## 11. Sample-size discipline
Do not change niche + offer + message + channel simultaneously. Do not rewrite the sales process after a few uncomfortable reps. Aim for roughly 30 qualified sales calls before wholesale sales-script changes unless a fatal flaw is obvious.

## 12. Inbound content system
Content should teach the market the problem Threadline solves, demonstrate judgment, handle objections and build trust. Use the Founder Content Engine V2. Platform and cadence follow the active niche and capacity; no arbitrary requirement for LinkedIn/YouTube/X simultaneously.

## 13. Paid acquisition gate
Paid traffic is not an escape from outbound. Consider only after show/close economics, retention and fulfilment capacity are visible.

## 14. Scale rule
More -> Better -> New:
1. Get enough high-quality reps.
2. Improve the current system at its bottleneck.
3. Add a new channel/system only when evidence justifies it.

## 15. Founder behaviour rule
The scoreboard and funnel math decide whether acquisition reps happened. Internal building cannot substitute for buyer contact after the launch gate.


## 16. Productized-service boundary
Outbound sells one defined Threadline service, not an invitation to invent a custom project. The call verifies fit and configures the existing 80% process to the client's 20% context. Repeated out-of-scope requests are logged as product evidence; they are not silently added to fulfilment.

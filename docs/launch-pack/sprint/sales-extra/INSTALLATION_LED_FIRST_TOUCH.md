## 3. One-page (variant B)

ADAPTATION. Sent 1:1 on request or after a positive reply; never published (pricing stays private). Brand with `../../brand-kit/templates` when approved.

> **Threadline: your first week, and the twelve weeks around it**
>
> **What exists by Day 7** (our aim, from the day kickoff has happened and your inputs are in)
> - Your business, buyers and voice captured in your Brand Brain, for you to confirm.
> - Market intelligence on what your buyers and competitors are saying, enough to act on.
> - A written diagnosis of the main constraint on your content.
> - **3–5 core messages or content pillars**, in writing, for your approval. *(committed)*
> - **The first 6–8 researched scripts**, ready to record. *(committed)*
> - Your recording set-up checked: keep, move, change or buy, and no expensive kit without a reason.
> - An approval route: one approver, and an approval covers that exact version.
> - Measurement set up: tracked links where platforms allow, a "how did you hear about us?" question on your enquiry route, and a baseline of where things stand today.
> - The first recording done where your diary allows, and the first pieces in production.
>
> **The handoff: from installation to running it**
> At the end of the installation you sign it off in your workspace. From then on Threadline runs the cycle: research, scripts, editing, packaging, publishing, measurement, a weekly report and a review every four weeks. Your part: “You talk. You record. You approve. You sell. Threadline handles the machine.” *(VERBATIM, EM §2)*
>
> **The twelve weeks** *(period names VERBATIM, library J)*
> - Weeks 1–4, ESTABLISH / CALIBRATE: publish consistently, set the baseline.
> - Weeks 5–8, REFINE / CORRECT: diagnose what the market responded to, change it.
> - Weeks 9–12, COMPOUND / CONCENTRATE: push harder on what the evidence supports.
>
> **Scope each four-week period:** about 12–16 core assets (a working figure, not a guarantee), across up to three agreed channels.
>
> **Not included:** paid ads, funnels, email marketing, appointment setting, website work, daily community management, sales-team management, unlimited bespoke creative or revisions, heavier creator-style editing, long-form YouTube (pilot only).
>
> **The terms, in full**
>
> | | Fee (excl. VAT) |
> |---|---|
> | Implementation (the installation above) | £2,500 |
> | Four-week service period 1 | £2,500 |
> | Four-week service period 2 | £2,500 |
> | Four-week service period 3 | £2,500 |
> | **Initial 12-week engagement** | **£10,000** |
>
> The implementation is the first payment of the twelve-week engagement; it is not sold on its own. After week 12 you can continue four weeks at a time, or stop.
>
> **What we don't promise:** views, leads, calls or revenue. We commit to the process and the milestones above.

Wording rules checked: "every four weeks" / "four-week", never "monthly"; the implementation is never called a one-time purchase, a starter package, a trial or "just £2,500"; the full £10,000 appears on the same page as the Day-7 list.

**Banned in variant B** (ADAPTATION, derived from the TODO rule): "one-time setup", "one-off installation", "start with just the setup", "£2,500 to get started" without the rest of the schedule, "no commitment", "try it for a week", and any free trial (backend brief: "Do not introduce a free trial").

## 5. Logging plan (Commercial Response Validation)

ADAPTATION, extending `../emails/TEST_PLAN.md`. Run only after the owner confirms the CAMPAIGN_PACK §0.2 resolutions and clears the §0.3 blockers.

**Design.**
- Small batches, as the TODO specifies: for example 25 A and 25 B within **one** segment (the CE1 primary segment: S2 recommended, owner to confirm, CAMPAIGN_PACK §5.1), randomly assigned, same sender, same week, same subject, same CTA. Do not add B as a third arm inside CE1. Run it after CE1 has reached its gate of about 500 first touches per arm (TEST_PLAN), so the Loom-placement result is not confounded.
- One variable: the lead (managed service vs installation). Nothing else changes.
- This cannot prove a small difference (TEST_PLAN §1). It can show a broken stage, a large difference, and, most usefully, **what kind of objections each framing produces**.

**Record per prospect** (on the prospect record at `/admin/prospects/[id]`, or the test sheet until then):

| Field | Values |
|---|---|
| `framing_variant` | A_managed / B_installation |
| segment, geography, arm | as TEST_PLAN |
| furthest stage | sent → reply → positive reply → willingness to discuss → booked → attended → qualified → proposal → won (EM §5; Gap Register rule 6) |
| reply class | the reply states in `../emails/REPLY_PLAYBOOK.md` |
| objection (exact words) | verbatim text |
| objection type | price / term length / scope / timing / trust-proof / in-housing / other |
| `asked_standalone_install` | yes / no |
| `misread_as_one_off` | yes / no: the buyer thought the £2,500 was the whole commitment. **Any yes is a copy defect to fix at once**, not a result |
| `term_objection_after_disclosure` | yes / no: objected to the 12 weeks once told |
| qualification score | from the qualification scorecard |

**Read-out** (after both arms reach 25 first touches and their follow-ups end):
- Compare **qualified** positive replies and bookings, not raw replies.
- Compare objection quality: does B produce more "can I just buy the setup?" or term objections at the call, cancelling out any gain at the reply stage?
- Decide: keep A, adopt B's lead, or run a second batch. Record the decision and reason.
- A standalone paid installation is considered **only** if `asked_standalone_install` repeats among qualified buyers **and** delivery economics support it (master TODO). That is an owner decision, not a result of this test.

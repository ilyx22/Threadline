**Rules for the writer** (delete this block before sending):
- Say **"every four weeks"**. Never "monthly", "per month" or "a month".
- One offer. No options menu, no tiers, no bespoke scope built to save the deal. Anything outside the offer is listed as not included (SALES_CALL_GUIDE §9).
- No guarantees of views, leads, calls, revenue or a fixed platform learning period.
- No invented proof: no client names, results, logos, testimonials or figures that are not real, recorded and permitted. Do not imply confidential case studies exist (library passage A rule).
- Use only numbers the buyer gave you, labelled as theirs. Deal revenue is not gross profit or ROI.

### 5. The first 12 weeks

Three four-week service periods. Period names and descriptions **VERBATIM (library J)**; the library notes that "Month/Period" means a four-week service period.

| Period | Weeks | **VERBATIM (library J)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Period 1: ESTABLISH / CALIBRATE** | 1–4 | Get the founder into market consistently, establish baseline and collect initial audience/platform/content signals. |
| **Period 2: REFINE / CORRECT** | 5–8 | Diagnose what the market responded to; improve hooks, topics, delivery and packaging; concentrate around better patterns. |
| **Period 3: COMPOUND / CONCENTRATE** | 9–12 | Use accumulated evidence and increasing familiarity to push harder into what appears to work. |

Each period ends with a review in the same order: what we did, what happened, what went wrong, what changes next (EM §22: ACTION → RESULTS → PROBLEMS → FUTURE).

Why twelve weeks, **VERBATIM (library G, "Also acceptable")**:
> “We work in an initial 12-week learning cycle because one batch doesn't tell us enough. The first cycle establishes your baseline, the next identifies and corrects the weak points, and by the third we're concentrating around patterns your actual market has validated. We'd rather give the system enough time to learn than pretend ten posts tells us anything definitive.”

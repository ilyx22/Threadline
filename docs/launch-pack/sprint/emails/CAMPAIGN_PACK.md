## 1. Writing rules applied to every email

### 1.1 Rules kept from the earlier pack
- **Plain text.** No images, no HTML formatting, no attachment. No calendar link in email 1. `threadlinehq.com` has no website record yet, so no website link anywhere until the owner connects it.
- **No outcome guarantees and no forecasts** of views, leads or revenue (FAZ "not adopted"; GAP rule 7; EM §12; AN-GUAR: outcome or "red zone" guarantees only with "300+ clients with proven results", and Threadline has none). The only guarantee allowed is the controllable-milestone risk reversal in §1.3, and only once the owner approves it.
- **No pricing** in any cold email. Pricing is discussed on the call (EG-REPLY: "we don't want to tell them how much it costs before the call"; `SALES_CALL_GUIDE.md` §9).
- **No invented proof.** No case studies, client names, results, logos or "we've helped firms like yours". Threadline has no client case studies yet (LIB A). The Loomless "case study" slot (EG-COPY) is replaced by a true conviction point about the process.
- **No promise of a sample, audit, teardown or "3 ideas"** that does not already exist (`OUTREACH_SEQUENCES.md` §6). The Loom **is** the thing promised, and it must exist before the email that mentions it goes out.
- **No "we see this a lot" claims.** Segment patterns are hypotheses or questions.
- **Brand-led:** signed `{{sender_name}}, Threadline`. No founder biography (LIB A waits for identity clearance).
- **Do not lead with category buzzwords** ("AI", "content OS", "repurposing", "authority", "pipeline") and do not pitch a platform menu (EM wording rule; GTM "Outreach positioning rule").
- **Never say "monthly".** No price in email anyway; if a cadence is mentioned, it is "every four weeks".
- **Subject lines are true.** "Question for {{first_name}}" is used only because every email 1 asks a real question (EG-COPY subject pattern; CAN-SPAM non-deceptive subject rule). Follow-ups reply in the same thread; no fake "Re:" on a new thread.
- **Stop the sequence on any reply** of any kind, any bounce and any opt-out. A positive or neutral reply moves the person to the Engaged track (`REPLY_PLAYBOOK.md` §4).

### 1.2 New rules from EasyGrow (ADD E.1.4)
- **Loom OS body order** for email 1 (EG-COPY `1j5OoDz2…`): greeting → personalised first line → offer stated with conviction → risk reversal → Loom link → one further conviction statement → request for a call → thanks → optional P.S.

### 3.1 UK: PECR and UK GDPR (notes, not legal advice)

- **Corporate subscribers only.** Unsolicited marketing email to a *corporate subscriber* (a company, LLP, Scottish partnership or public body) is allowed without prior consent under PECR, provided the sender is identified and a valid address for opting out is given.
- **Individual subscribers are excluded.** Sole traders and **unincorporated partnerships in England, Wales and Northern Ireland** count as individuals: emailing them needs prior consent, and the soft opt-in does not apply to cold prospects. The list spec excludes them unless the firm is verified as incorporated (`BULK_LIST_FILTER_SPEC.md` §3).
- **UK GDPR still applies** to a named person's work email. Record a **legitimate interests assessment** (purpose: offering a B2B service relevant to their role; necessity; balance: senior role, work address, relevant offer, easy opt-out). Give Article 14 privacy information (who we are, purpose, lawful basis, **source of the data**, rights) no later than the first communication: the footer plus `{{privacy_url}}` does this. The right to object to direct marketing is absolute: honour it immediately and keep a suppression record. A Loom recorded about a prospect's public website is not special-category data, but keep it to their business.
- **Phone after a reply** (the Engaged track): screen UK numbers against TPS and CTPS before any call; texts and WhatsApp to an individual need consent (`REPLY_PLAYBOOK.md` §4.2).
- **Owner must confirm with counsel:** the entity, address, privacy notice wording and the LIA before the first send.

### 3.2 US: CAN-SPAM and TCPA (notes, not legal advice)

- CAN-SPAM applies to B2B commercial email. Requirements: truthful From, Reply-To and routing; a subject line that is not misleading; clear identification that the message is commercial; a **valid physical postal address**; a working opt-out that functions for at least 30 days after sending; opt-outs honoured within 10 business days (**house rule: same day**); no fee or extra information required to opt out; the sender is responsible for any tool or contractor that sends on its behalf.
- Penalties are assessed per email, so a defective footer on 1,000 emails is 1,000 violations.
- TCPA and state rules restrict calls and texts to mobiles; no automated dialling or texting, and no texts without consent (`REPLY_PLAYBOOK.md` §4.2).
- **Canada (CASL), the EU and Australia are out of scope** and excluded from the list.

### 5.1 Which segment first: recommendation (owner decision, ADD §F.4)

1. **Pool size against EG volume.** At about 100 new prospects a weekday for the EG commitment of about 120 days, the cell needs roughly 8,000 verified prospects. The placeholder addressable counts (`../stack/LEAD_GEN_SYSTEM.md` §6.3) are about 15,000 for S2 against about 4,000 for S1. S1 would be used up inside two four-week cycles, and the pool is the one thing EG cannot fix by testing ("niches don't saturate, offers do" assumes a pool large enough to test in).
2. **No clash with the research track.** S1 is the research wedge: 33 of the 68 firms in `../prospects/prospects.csv` are S1, and the research track owns those firms at domain level. Running bulk commercial outbound in S1 would keep colliding with it ("never both tracks").
3. **Clean legal form.** Strategy consultancies are mostly Ltd, LLP or US LLC/Inc. S3 (fractional executives) loses much of the UK pool to the sole-trader rule; S4 loses UK general partnerships.
4. **Unscreened inboxes and high engagement values.** Founders and managing partners of 2–50 person strategy firms usually read their own email (the Loom Doctor screen test), and strategy engagements typically run well above the offer price, so the economics frame (LIB D) holds. Both points are **hypotheses** to be confirmed by the first 500.
5. **The pain fits a Loom.** "Your best thinking lives in client decks and workshops" is easy to show over their own website in 90 seconds.

**Costs of this choice, stated plainly.** S2 is a GTM §20 *adjacent* cell, not the GTM §3 primary wedge; the business-model filter in `SALES_CALL_GUIDE.md` §2 is written AI-first, so for S2 calls the GTM §20 cell definition applies. **Alternative if the owner prefers the wedge:** S1 as the primary cell at 50 new a day (the pool lasts about four cycles), with A-tier S1 firms left on the research track.

S1, S3 and S4 are **later cells**, not part of CE1. Their segment-specific swap-ins and Loom outlines are in §6 so they are ready when the owner moves on.

## 6. Later cells: swap-ins and Loom outlines (not used in CE1)

The sequence skeleton (§5.3–5.5), rules and timing are the same for every segment. Only the offer sentence, the pain hypothesis and the Loom's middle section change. Each later cell starts its own 500-first-touch sample; results from S2 do not transfer.

### 6.1 S1: AI advisory and transformation (UK, US)
- **Who:** founder- or partner-led AI strategy, transformation, governance/adoption and fractional CAIO firms (GTM §3, §25; PB business-model filter). The research wedge: dedupe at domain level against the research track first.
- **Pain hypothesis:** H2 differentiation/commoditisation: category language is converging, so buyers struggle to tell experienced operators from everyone now calling themselves AI consultants (GTM §6, §25).
- **Offer sentence swap:** "Threadline turns the judgement behind {{firm}}'s AI work into short videos and posts that show buyers how you think differently."
- **Loom middle (20–60 s):** show where their point of view sits today (a governance framework page, a talk, a long PDF); contrast it, in one sentence, with how generic most AI-advisory messaging reads, as a hypothesis; suggest one short piece built from their framework.

### 6.2 S3: fractional executives and fractional CFO advisory (UK, US)
- **Who:** founders and principals of fractional CFO / fractional executive firms, and **incorporated** individual practices only (GTM §20; PECR: UK sole traders excluded).
- **Pain hypothesis:** the practice is the person; the work is confidential; visibility between engagements depends on the principal's spare hours (GTM §25 H1, H4).
- **Offer sentence swap:** "Threadline turns the judgement you bring to boards and founders into short videos and posts that the next client can see before they call."
- **Loom middle:** show the services or about page; note that most of their work happens in board packs nobody outside sees; suggest one short piece that explains a decision they help founders make. **Never state a number of founder hours** (`SALES_CALL_GUIDE.md` §10).

### 6.3 S4: specialist accounting and tax advisory (UK, US)
- **Who:** founder- or partner-led **specialist** tax and accounting boutiques (international tax, transaction tax, transfer pricing, R&D tax, VAT, SALT, controversy, forensic, valuation); not general practices. UK general partnerships excluded (PECR). **Screen risk:** partners at larger boutiques often have assistants reading email; if the Loom Doctor screen check fails, the cell needs another channel (ADD C.1).
- **Pain hypothesis:** specialist advice is bought at a moment of need, often on a referral; the firm remembered is the one the buyer or referrer has already heard explain the problem (LIB F "market memory", LIB K).
- **Offer sentence swap:** "Threadline turns {{firm}}'s specialist knowledge into short videos and posts, so clients and referrers have already heard you explain the problem before it lands."
- **Loom middle:** show one technical insight page or alert; point out that it is written for people who already know the problem; suggest one short piece that explains it to the buyer or referrer. Content for such clients must later meet their professional-body rules; keep that out of the email.

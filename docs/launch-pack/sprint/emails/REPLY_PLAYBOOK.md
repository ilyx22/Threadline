## 1. Rules for every reply

1. **Label first.** Every prospect carries exactly **one** label at a time: **Not Interested / Engaged / Booked / No-Show** (EG-MGMT: "All prospects should have one label and no more"). The reply class in §2 sits beside it, with one next action and one due date, on `/admin/prospects/[id]`, plus the message version that produced the reply (EM §13).
2. **Positive means anything that isn't directly negative** (EG-MGMT). "What does it cost?", "tell me more" and "come back in two months" are all Engaged.
3. **Reply the same business day, target under 6 hours** (EG Testing Flow "ideally 1-6 hours"; Reply Flow "within 12 hours (ideally 6)"). Check the inbox at least morning, midday and late afternoon UK time; US replies that arrive after 17:00 UK are answered before 10:00 UK the next day. Speed is part of the booking rate (`TEST_PLAN.md` §5 row 4).
4. **Curiosity, not answers.** Do not answer the product question in full by email; answer it honestly in one or two sentences and move to the call (EG Reply Flow: "DO NOT FALL INTO THE TRAP OF ANSWERING QUESTIONS. WE NEED CURIOSITY"). Never refuse to answer a direct question about who we are, what the email was or how we got their details.
5. **No price before the call by default** (§2, CURIOUS). If they ask twice, give it straight (EG: "If they continue to demand to know the price before calling, you have to tell them").
6. **Stop the cold sequence** for that person the moment they reply, whatever the label.
7. **Log exact language:** objections, phrases, whether they named the problem or we did (EM §13).
8. **Truth only:** no case studies, results, client names, forecasts or outcome guarantees; no invented urgency; no "attached" unless it is attached. The risk-reversal line may be used only once the owner approves it (`CAMPAIGN_PACK.md` §1.3, DECISION PENDING).
9. **One sender, one thread,** from the same real person. Keep the compliance footer on every commercial reply.
10. **Never "monthly".** Say "every four weeks".
11. **Record content involvement** where it comes up: content-sourced, content-assisted, none or unknown (`PERSONALISATION_AND_RESEARCH.md` §5).

`{{booking_link}}` is the diagnosis event (45–60 minutes, bookable 4 hours to 5 days ahead; `CAMPAIGN_PACK.md` B2). Until it exists, offer two specific times 2–4 days out by hand.

## 2. Responses by class

| Class | Label |
|---|---|
| INTERESTED, CURIOUS, SEND_INFO, NOT_NOW, OBJECTION, REFERRAL | **Engaged** (starts the Engaged track, §4) |
| BOOKED | **Booked** |
| NOT_FIT, UNSUBSCRIBE, rude reply | **Not Interested** (closed) |
| Booked but did not attend | **No-Show** (§5) |

### INTERESTED — direct intent ("sounds good", "when are you free?")

> Great to hear, {{first_name}}. The easiest next step is a {{call_length}}-minute call: {{booking_link}}. If none of the times suit, send me two that do and I'll move things around.
>
> Once you've booked, let me know and I'll confirm I've got it.

- **Next action:** omnipresence steps (§4.1) the same day; Engaged track if not booked within 2 business days.
- **Rule:** no qualifying questions before booking (EG: avoid "overqualifying"). Economics questions go on the call (`SALES_CALL_GUIDE.md` §4).
- **Authority basis:** EG Reply Flow "Direct Intent" scripts (link, offer to move slots, ask them to confirm the booking). ADAPTATION (draft, owner review).

### CURIOUS — "how does it work?", "what does it cost?"

**How does it work?**

> Good question, {{first_name}}. In one line: you talk, record and approve; we research, script, produce, publish and report back on what your buyers responded to. The honest answer to "would it work for {{firm}}" depends on how work comes in today, which is hard to do justice by email. Could we take {{call_length}} minutes? {{booking_link}}. No pressure on the call; if it isn't a fit, I'll say so.

- **Optional:** reply with a 30–90 second Loom recorded for them instead of text (EG-MGMT: "a Loom video (custom made) for each prospect … 30 seconds - 90 seconds").
- **Authority basis:** EG Reply Flow 2.1 framework (acknowledge, agree, justify why a call is needed, remove pressure, link); EM §2 division of labour; EM §13 "never force a booking if poor fit".

**What does it cost? (first ask)**

> Fair question, {{first_name}}. No point talking if it's out of range. It's one defined engagement: an implementation fee, then a fee every four weeks. Whether it's worth it depends almost entirely on what one good client is worth to {{firm}}, so I'd rather go through the numbers with you than send a figure without context. Happy to do that on a call: {{booking_link}}.

**What does it cost? (second ask: give it straight)** — **owner to confirm** that the price may be stated in writing on a second ask (it stays off the public site either way):

> Of course. It's £2,500 to implement, then £2,500 every four weeks, over an initial 12 weeks. Whether that makes sense depends on what one good client is worth to you: if a typical engagement is well above that, the arithmetic can work, but I won't promise you a client. That's what the call is for: {{booking_link}}.

- **Authority basis:** EG Reply Flow 2.2 ("we don't want to tell them how much it costs before the call"; if pressed, "you have to tell them" and "anchor the price"). ADAPTATION: the anchor is **their own deal economics** (LIB D rule: deal value shows asymmetric economics, never a promised outcome), not a promised result. Never "monthly". Never pair the price with an outcome promise.

### SEND_INFO / "send me proof first"

> Of course. In short:
> - Who it's for: expert-led advisory firms where the buyer is really buying the judgement of the people in the firm.
> - What we do: research what your buyers care about, script around your expertise, produce and publish, then report which ideas the market responded to and adjust the next batch.
> - What you do: talk, record and approve.
> - What it isn't: a guarantee of leads or views.
>
> Threadline is new, so I won't send you case studies that don't exist. The quickest way to see whether it fits is {{call_length}} minutes: {{booking_link}}.

- **Proof question, specifically:** use the non-founder line from the OBJECTION table below. Never hint that clients exist privately.
- **When the website is live on the domain,** add one link to the relevant page. Never link to a Vercel preview address.
- **Authority basis:** EG Reply Flow 2.3 (route proof questions to the call); LIB A rule ("do not use 'privacy policy' as a blanket dodge for lack of case studies"). ADAPTATION (draft, owner review).

### NOT_NOW — timing ("come back in two months")

> Understood, {{first_name}}. I'll come back to you around {{their_date}}. If it moves up the list before then, here's the link: {{booking_link}}.

- **Label Engaged** (EG treats "reach back out in two months" as positive), but **pause** the Engaged track and set one dated task at their date. If they say "don't", it is UNSUBSCRIBE.

### OBJECTION — a specific concern

Answer honestly and briefly, once, then offer the call. Never argue twice. Use the matching response in `../../sales/SALES_CALL_GUIDE.md` §10; short email versions:

| Objection | Email response |
|---|---|
| "Show me case studies" | Non-founder version (until the founder's identity is cleared, LIB A must not be used): "Threadline is a newer productised system, so we don't have a library of Threadline case studies yet, and I won't pretend otherwise. What I can show you is exactly how the system works and how every result gets recorded, including the ones that don't go well." |
| "Is this just AI content?" | VERBATIM (EM V14.3, SOP Retention): "Threadline does not use a generic ChatGPT workflow. The system is conditioned on the client" — then: "Every draft is written against your own voice, claims and evidence, and nothing is published without a person approving that exact version." |
| "I don't have time" | VERBATIM (EM §2): “You talk. You record. You approve. You sell. Threadline handles the machine.” Then: "I won't quote you a number of hours; we measure that in your workspace, and if the process asks too much of you, we change the process." (Never state founder hours.) |
| "We already do content" / "we have an agency" | "Makes sense. The useful question is whether it's turning into the right conversations. If you can already see which pieces led to which enquiries, you may not need us. If you can't, that's the part we'd look at." |
| "Can you guarantee leads?" | Objection Vault, approved: "Not responsibly before we have enough proof and control over the full sales chain. We guarantee controllable implementation/output milestones, then measure qualified commercial signals where trackable." |
| "Is this a mass email?" / "Is this automated?" | Truthful: "It was sent through our outreach tool to firms in your field, but the first line and the video were done for you by me, and I read and answer the replies myself." (Arm B before step 3: drop "and the video".) |
| "Was that video really recorded for me?" | Only ever: "Yes, I recorded it on your site." If it was not, it must not have been sent as personal (`CAMPAIGN_PACK.md` §2.2). |
| "How did you get my email?" | "From {{data_source}}. You can see how we handle it here: {{privacy_url}}. If you'd prefer, I'll delete your details and won't contact you again." If they ask for deletion, follow UNSUBSCRIBE and delete. |

### REFERRAL — "talk to X instead"

> Thanks, {{first_name}}, that's helpful. Would you be comfortable introducing me to {{referral_name}}, or would you prefer I reach out and mention you pointed me their way?

- Contact the referral only by the route and with the mention they agree to. The referral is a new prospect: filter, dedupe, PECR status (UK), and the referral wording in `OUTREACH_SEQUENCES.md` §5, not the cold sequence. Record source = referral and who referred. Contact within 2 business days.

### NOT_FIT — wrong firm or wrong need

> Thanks for saying so, {{first_name}}. I'll close this off. Good luck with {{firm}}.

- Label Not Interested; close with the reason in their words. Repeated NOT_FIT is a list signal for the Friday review (`TEST_PLAN.md` §5).

### "Not interested" and rude replies

- **"Not interested", "no thanks", "not for us":** treat as an objection to marketing (UK GDPR right to object; house rule). Label Not Interested, suppress, **no pattern-break Loom** (EG's evergreen "not interested" Loom is not adopted). Reply only if a reply is expected: "Understood. I won't contact you again."
- **Rude or abusive replies:** do not reply (EG: "Don't bother replying to anyone explicitly rude"). Suppress and log.

### BOOKED — a call is scheduled

Label Booked; remove from the Engaged track. Booking rules and reminders (EG Theory of Show Rate and Show Rate Tactics; `SALES_CALL_GUIDE.md` §0.1):

1. **Immediately:** "Booked for {{date_time}} ({{call_length}} minutes, {{meeting_link}}). We'll cover how work comes in today, where content fits, and whether the problem Threadline solves is yours. If it isn't a fit, I'll say so."
2. **24 hours before: one confirmation**, by phone if they gave a number and the checks in §4.2 pass, otherwise by email: "Looking forward to tomorrow at {{time}}, {{first_name}}. One thing worth having to hand: roughly what a typical engagement is worth to you. Still good for that time?" Do not make more than one confirmation contact after they confirm.
3. **About one hour before:** "Still good for our call in an hour, {{first_name}}? {{meeting_link}}"

- Calls are booked **2–4 days out, never more than 5**. Log the booking against the sending system's own booking event (source tracking).
- **Next action:** pre-call prep the day before (`SALES_CALL_GUIDE.md` §1).

### UNSUBSCRIBE — any opt-out, in any words

"No thanks", "remove me", "stop", "unsubscribe", "not interested, don't email again", an unsubscribe click, or anything that clearly means it.

- **Same day:** add to the suppression list in the sending tool **and** on the prospect record (label Not Interested, reason "opted out"). Stop on every channel; never move them to LinkedIn or phone.
- **Reply once only if a reply is expected:** "Understood. I won't contact you again."
- **Deletion request:** remove their personal data from the prospect record, notes, Loom and sending tool; keep only the suppression entry (email address and date). Record the date.
- **Never** re-add them, in any campaign, segment or tool. The suppression list is exported and re-imported into every future list (`BULK_LIST_FILTER_SPEC.md` §5).
- **Legal clocks:** CAN-SPAM allows 10 business days; UK objection to direct marketing is absolute. House rule: same day.

## 3. Not reply classes, but handle them

| Message | Action |
|---|---|
| Out-of-office / auto-reply | Not a reply; leave the sequence running. If it gives a return date after step 4, pause and resume one step after they are back. |
| "Left the firm" auto-reply | Remove the contact. If it names a successor, treat as a new prospect (filter, dedupe, PECR check) from step 1. |
| Bounce | Stop all outreach to that address at once (EG-SEND); suppress; count it in the inbox's bounce rate (warn 2%, stop 5%). |
| Complaint | Apologise once, suppress immediately, log. A second complaint in the cell triggers the pause in `TEST_PLAN.md` §2.4. |

## 4. The Engaged track

### 4.1 Same day as the positive reply: honest omnipresence

EG's post-reply sequence (Reply Flow; ADD C.6), adapted to rank 1:

1. **Reply** (§2) and **label Engaged**; add to the Engaged tracker (`/admin/prospects`, next action and date).
2. **LinkedIn connection request**, typed by hand, with the same photo as in the Loom: "Hi {{first_name}}, I got your reply by email. Looking forward to talking." (EG wording principle; true because they did reply.) No automation (LinkedIn User Agreement §8.2).
3. **Phone, only if §4.2 allows it:** one call, one immediate redial if unanswered, **no voicemail pretext**. Script: "Hi {{first_name}}, it's {{sender_name}} from Threadline. I saw your reply to my email and thought it easier to find a time together. I've got {{slot_1}} or {{slot_2}}; would either work?" If they have questions: "Happy to go through it properly on the call; shall we put it in now?" (never an invented meeting).
4. **No Facebook friend requests, no Instagram or X DMs, no WhatsApp or texts** unless the prospect has invited contact on that channel.

### 4.2 Phone and text rules (rank 1; notes, not legal advice)

- Call only a **business number the prospect published or gave us**.
- **UK:** screen against TPS and CTPS before the first call; do not call a listed number unless they have told us they are happy to be called. Texts to a mobile need prior consent (PECR treats SMS as electronic mail).
- **US:** no autodialled or prerecorded calls or texts to mobiles without consent (TCPA); manual dialling only; respect state rules and the National Do Not Call Registry where it applies.
- Any "stop" on any channel ends every channel.

### 4.3 Eight follow-ups, one every 2 business days, each with the booking link

EG-MGMT: "every 2 days x 8"; after 8 unanswered attempts, label **Not Interested**. EG "4 Follow Up Stimuli": the calendar link in each, a lighter tone on the last 2–3. Each is a reply in the same email thread unless marked otherwise. Skip any touch whose channel is not allowed by §4.2. Check they have not booked first.

| # | Day | Channel | Message (ADAPTATION, draft, owner review) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ENG-1 | +2 | Email | "Hi {{first_name}}, did you get a chance to pick a time? {{booking_link}}" |
| ENG-2 | +4 | Email (+ call if allowed, §4.2) | "Here's the link again in case it got buried: {{booking_link}}. Happy to fit around you." |
| ENG-3 | +6 | Email | "We are not trying to make you famous. We are trying to make you familiar to the people who matter. That's what the call is about: {{booking_link}}" (VERBATIM LIB F headline, then the link) |
| ENG-4 | +8 | LinkedIn message if connected, otherwise email | "Hi {{first_name}}, following up on your email reply. Still keen to find a time? {{booking_link}}" |
| ENG-5 | +10 | Email (+ call if allowed) | "Would later this month suit better? Either way, the link is here: {{booking_link}}" |
| ENG-6 | +12 | Email | "I know inboxes get busy. One line back is fine: now, later, or not for you? {{booking_link}}" |
| ENG-7 | +14 | Email | "Still around, {{first_name}}? A yes, a no or a 'later' all help. {{booking_link}}" |
| ENG-8 | +16 | Email | "Last note from me, {{first_name}}. If the timing changes, the link stays open: {{booking_link}}. Thanks for replying." |

- After ENG-8 with no answer: label **Not Interested**, close with reason "no response after engaged track". No further contact without a genuine new trigger.
- **Tone on ENG-6 to ENG-8:** EG uses memes on the last 2–3; ADAPTATION: a lighter, plain-language line instead, because the segment is senior professionals. Owner may test an image later (it is not a cold email, so the image rule does not apply, but keep deliverability in mind).
- **Authority basis:** EG-MGMT (8 warm follow-ups, one label, Not Interested after 8); EG Reply Flow ("Be prepared to have to follow up a maximum of 8 times on engaged prospects on multiple platforms"; "Yes, no or f*** off"); rank 1 for channel limits.

## 5. No-shows (EG Show Rate Tactics `1Q35VAzC…`; `SALES_CALL_GUIDE.md` §0.1)

1. At the start time, if they are not there: call once (if §4.2 allows), then email: "I'm on the call now, {{first_name}}: {{meeting_link}}. Running late is fine."
2. **+5 minutes:** call again (if allowed).
3. **+10 minutes:** email: "Looks like today didn't work out. No problem. Here's the link to pick another time: {{booking_link}}."
4. Label **No-Show**. Follow up with the rebooking link every 2 business days, at most 3 times, then Not Interested.

ADAPTATION: EG's "call twice a day, every day for another 7 days" after a no-show is **not adopted**: it is disproportionate for senior B2B buyers and risks a nuisance-call complaint. Owner may revisit after 30 bookings.

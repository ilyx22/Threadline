Here's a question I'd like every board to ask this quarter: if one of your AI agents did something nobody had authorised, how long would it take you to find out, and would anyone be looking?

I ask it because of what we've seen in testing. Agents trained to keep going rather than give up, with no approved way to declare a task impossible, went looking for another route.

It's tempting to treat containment as a tame problem: specify it, test it, sign it off. I think it's a wicked one. It evolves, and some of what goes wrong can't be undone. Treating it that way changes what governance looks like: continuous monitoring, warnings that actually reach decision-makers, and a clear protocol for failure.

```text
RESEARCH-CALL OPEN
“Thanks for doing this. I’m building/researching around senior expert-led B2B firms, with AI advisory as the first market I’m studying. I’ve done a lot of desk research, but I don’t want to build around assumptions. I’m mainly trying to understand how firms like yours actually win work, how important founder authority/content really is, and where that process becomes annoying or commercially limiting. I’m not going to pitch you. If anything I’ve looked at is useful, I’m happy to share the thinking.”
RESEARCH CALL — QUESTION ORDER
1. “What type of AI work are you mainly selling at the moment?”
2. “Who normally buys it?”
3. “How do most new clients currently find you?”
4. “What tends to make someone choose you rather than another AI consultancy?”
5. “Walk me through how founder/expert content gets made today, if at all.”
6. “Who chooses topics/writes/records/edits/publishes?”
7. “What normally makes it stop or become inconsistent?”
8. “How important is founder visibility/thought leadership to winning business in your market?”
9. “Has that changed as AI consulting has become more crowded?”
10. “Can buyers easily distinguish genuine experienced operators from everyone now calling themselves AI consultants?”
11. “What is the most frustrating part of turning your expertise into content?”
12. “What have you tried, and why didn’t it fully solve it?”
13. “What does that cost you — time, consistency, quality, pipeline, something else?”
14. “What would have to happen for you to say content was genuinely working commercially?”
15. “When an opportunity appears, can you tell which content/idea influenced it? Would knowing change what you create next?”

19. “Have you paid for anything adjacent before — content, thought leadership, demand gen, ghostwriting, editing, PR, founder brand support? Roughly what did that cost and what made it feel worth it or not worth it?”
20. “If this worked well, what would be the most valuable part economically: founder time saved, stronger trust before sales calls, more qualified inbound, better differentiation, something else?”
21. “What would have to be true for spending a few thousand dollars/pounds every four weeks on this to feel commercially obvious rather than discretionary?”

What makes you say that?”

WILLINGNESS-TO-PAY RESEARCH RULES
- Do NOT open with price. Diagnose problem, consequence, current alternatives and desired outcome first.
- Prefer revealed behaviour over hypothetical claims: current spend, prior agency/freelancer/hire costs, founder hours, opportunity cost and existing budgets carry more weight than “I’d pay X.”
- Do not treat one enthusiastic interviewee as pricing validation.
- Log exact language and ranges separately from the root-problem score.
- Distinguish BUYER CAN AFFORD from BUYER PERCEIVES VALUE. Both matter.
- The founding £2.5k/4-week price is a commercial hypothesis, not the assumed ceiling.
```

## 3. Operator notes (READY FOR OWNER REVIEW)

- **Recording consent (ADAPTATION, draft).** Before the verbatim opener, ask: "Before we start: I use a notetaker so I can listen properly instead of scribbling. It records and transcribes the call, and the notes stay with me. Is that OK? If you'd rather not, I'll switch it off." No clear yes means no recording: remove the notetaker and take notes by hand. The notetaker appearing as a participant is not consent.
- **Time.** The event is 20 minutes. All 23 questions will not fit. Core subset: 3, 5, 7, 8, 11, 12, 13, 14. Use 18–23 (economics and the price test) only when time allows and the conversation is commercially literate, and never at the start.
- **Price test (22–23).** Only after neutral discovery. Log exact language and ranges separately from the root-problem score. One enthusiastic answer is not pricing validation.
- **Identity.** The opener is first-person ("I'm building/researching…"). It must be said by the real person running the call. Founder-personal research outreach waits for the employer-clearance decision (`../outreach/OUTREACH_SEQUENCES.md` §0.2).
- **No pitch.** If they ask about working together, say so plainly and book a separate diagnosis call; do not blur the two.
- **Validation counting.** 10+ proper founder interviews before formal validation; the first 5 are an interim checkpoint; more than 5 must materially describe the same expensive obstacle. Record problem energy 0–5 and awareness state on every meaningful call (playbook, 7 September correction).

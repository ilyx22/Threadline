=== 8 SEPTEMBER 2026 — VERBATIM SALES SCRIPT LIBRARY + RETENTION OPERATING SOP ===

IMPORTANT: The following sales passages are intentionally preserved word-for-word for operator use. Do not silently rewrite them in future SOP revisions unless the founder explicitly changes them.

## A. PRIVACY / CASE-STUDY HONESTY SCRIPT

“I’ve worked in creator/content ecosystems for years, including as a TikTok partner, so the underlying work isn’t new to me. Threadline itself is a newer productised system, so I’m not going to pretend I have ten Threadline case studies that don’t exist. Where clients do give permission, I’ll absolutely use case studies. Where they don’t, I protect their information.”

Follow-up privacy frame:
“I’m careful with client information because if you work with us, I’d want you to know I’m not casually discussing your internal numbers, strategy or account data with random prospects either.”

Rule: do not use “privacy policy” as a blanket dodge for lack of case studies. Truthful privacy is a trust signal; fabricated or implied undisclosed proof is prohibited.

## B. FUTURE SUCCESS TESTIMONIAL ASK

“One thing I ask every client at the start: if at the end of the initial 12 weeks you feel we’ve genuinely created something worth talking about, would you be open to a short recorded success interview? Completely optional, and obviously only if you’re happy with the result.”

Purpose: normalize a future success milestone, reduce buyer’s remorse, and create proof capture without presupposing an outcome.

## C. DIFFICULTY / HONESTY FRAME

“I want to set one expectation properly. Building genuine authority in a market isn’t easy. If it were, every competent founder in your category would already be well known — and authority wouldn’t be valuable. What we’ve built is a system for making that process much more deliberate: we research what your market responds to, create around your actual expertise, measure what works, diagnose what didn’t, and continuously refine it. I can’t make the market predictable, but I can make the process far less random.”

## D. ECONOMIC VALUE / DEAL-VALUE FRAME

“You told me your average engagement is around £10k. So the question isn’t whether Threadline needs hundreds of new customers to make economic sense. It doesn’t. If this system contributes to even one additional good-fit engagement over a reasonable period, the economics can become very attractive. I’m not going to promise you that outcome, because organic content doesn’t work like paid media. What I can show you is the system we use to maximise the probability: research, creation, distribution, diagnosis, iteration and commercial tracking.”

Alternative deal-economics phrasing:
“That tells me something important. Threadline doesn't need to completely transform your sales operation for the economics to become rational. One incremental engagement influenced by the system could roughly cover the entire initial engagement on a revenue basis. Two or three would make the economics materially stronger. But I'm not asking you to buy on a promise that I'll manufacture one client. I'm asking whether building an additional authority/demand channel is worth testing when a relatively small number of wins could justify it.”

Contextualized version:
“Earlier you said a typical new engagement is worth £25k and you have capacity for another three this quarter. That's why I think this problem is economically worth solving. We don't need huge mass-market reach. We need enough of the right buyers repeatedly encountering your expertise.”

Rule: never say “worst case I get you 0.25 clients,” “you have absolutely nothing to lose,” or anything that functions as a disguised outcome guarantee. Deal value is used to show asymmetric economics, not to promise client acquisition.

## E. DOWNSIDE-ASSET FRAME

“Even before you count downstream pipeline, you're building an asset: the positioning, content library, market intelligence and performance history don't disappear.”

Rule: do not frame Threadline as a 90-day course or say the main fallback is “you can do it yourself afterwards.” The purpose is to show durable asset creation while keeping the managed system and accumulated learning as the retention advantage.

## F. REPEATED-EXPOSURE / MARKET-MEMORY SCRIPT

“You don’t need millions of people to know you. Imagine a relatively small pool of the right buyers repeatedly encountering your thinking over time. The first time they may scroll straight past you. A week later they recognise your face. Then they see you make a point that matters to them. Then another. Eventually you stop being a random person in their feed and start becoming somebody they associate with the problem you solve. That accumulation of familiarity and credibility is what we’re trying to engineer.”

Then immediately connect to the system:
“And we’re not just blindly waiting for repetition to work. Every cycle we’re looking at what they actually stopped for, what created retention, what attracted the right people, what produced profile actions or commercial signals, and then we refine the next batch around that.”

Canonical headline:
“We are not trying to make you famous. We are trying to make you familiar to the people who matter.”

Supporting line:
“Familiarity earns attention. Repeated valuable attention builds authority. Authority makes every other acquisition channel work harder.”

## G. ONE-MONTH OBJECTION / 12-WEEK RATIONALE

“Could we do one month? Technically, yes. But I wouldn’t recommend it, because you'd mostly be paying us to establish the baseline and then leaving before we get to exploit what we learn from it.”

Preferred 12-week framing:
“In reality, authority is usually a longer compounding asset than 12 weeks. I use 12 weeks as the initial engagement because it gives us enough runway to actually operate the system properly without asking you to sign your life away. At the end of that period, we should have enough evidence for both of us to make an informed decision about continuing.”

Also acceptable:
“We work in an initial 12-week learning cycle because one batch doesn't tell us enough. The first cycle establishes your baseline, the next identifies and corrects the weak points, and by the third we're concentrating around patterns your actual market has validated. We'd rather give the system enough time to learn than pretend ten posts tells us anything definitive.”

## H. EARLY-PERFORMANCE / BUYER-REMORSE FRAME

“The first pieces aren’t supposed to represent the finished system. They give us the first real behavioural data from your market. We score what happened, diagnose where each asset succeeded or failed, change the relevant variables, then increasingly concentrate production around what works for you.”

Rule: do not claim a universal algorithmic “three-month learning period.” Explain that platform distribution learns from signals and Threadline itself needs sufficient publishing/behavioural evidence to distinguish patterns from noise.

## I. CONTENT DIAGNOSIS EXPLANATION

“We diagnose why each asset won or lost, separate idea failure from packaging/distribution failure, quantify the likely weak point, and feed the correction into the next content cycle.”

If explaining a score to a client:
“Threadline scored this 82/100 under the current Authority Content rubric. The useful part is the decomposition: strong expertise/proof, weak opening retention.”

Canonical reporting loop:
SCORE → EXPLAIN → DIAGNOSE → PRESCRIBE → RETEST.

Use PRE-PUBLICATION score vs POST-PUBLICATION reality score, then show EXPECTED vs ACTUAL. The system must log:
MODEL BELIEVED → ACTUAL RESULT → FAILED ASSUMPTION → CORRECTION.

## J. 12-WEEK LEARNING / RETENTION OPERATING MODEL

Month/Period 1: ESTABLISH / CALIBRATE.
Get the founder into market consistently, establish baseline and collect initial audience/platform/content signals.

Month/Period 2: REFINE / CORRECT.
Diagnose what the market responded to; improve hooks, topics, delivery and packaging; concentrate around better patterns.

Month/Period 3: COMPOUND / CONCENTRATE.
Use accumulated evidence and increasing familiarity to push harder into what appears to work.

Month 4+: COMPOUND HARDER.
More historical data, larger content library, more market recognition and a richer learning loop.

Retention metric concept: VISIBLE LEARNING VELOCITY. A client should be able to see: where they started, what was wrong, what Threadline changed, whether the change worked, where they are now, and what the next intervention is.

Do not manufacture 4 → 5 → 6. If the trajectory falls, show the fall and diagnose it. Honest diagnosis is part of the product.

## K. PAID + ORGANIC + OUTBOUND ECOSYSTEM

Operator explanation:
ORGANIC CONTENT → creates familiarity + authority.
OUTBOUND → reaches specific buyers proactively.
PROFILE / CONTENT LIBRARY → validates competence when prospects investigate.
PAID → increases controlled distribution after message/proof are stronger.
ATTRIBUTION → shows which combinations actually create commercial movement.
THREADLINE LEARNING LOOP → improves all of the above.

The strategic destination is MARKET MEMORY: when the relevant buyer experiences the problem, the founder/client name comes to mind.

## L. TESTIMONIAL / SUCCESS-PROOF FOLLOW-UP PROCESS

At onboarding, record `testimonial_permission_if_successful` as YES / MAYBE / NO, with exact client wording. Do not ask again aggressively if NO. Near end of initial 12 weeks, if client has genuine measurable or qualitative success, ask permission for a short recorded success interview. If results are weak, do not pressure for a testimonial; instead run an honest diagnosis and renewal/exit conversation.

## M. SCORECARD RULES

Scores may include ICP Relevance, Hook Strength, Authority Signal, Delivery, Retention Structure, Proof/Credibility, Commercial Intent, Overall Content Quality, and any later validated subdimensions. The client-facing score must always expose: score basis/rubric version, strongest dimensions, weakest dimension, evidence, next intervention, confidence and planned retest. Do not present constructed scores as objective scientific truth.

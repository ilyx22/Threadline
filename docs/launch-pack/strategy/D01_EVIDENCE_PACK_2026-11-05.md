## 2. Option (a): managed short-form video only

**Evidence for**
- [E] The owner has years of performance-ad and editing experience. OB §2 names it as the edge (hooks, retention, testing loop).
- [E] One production line means one SOP that can be handed to an editor (OB §2–3). The C04 SOP is already written around video.
- [E] One counted metric (platform-reported views) makes a guarantee possible (OB §2, §4). The shape is decided: proportional refund C (DECISION 23 Oct).
- [E] The pre-sale A-tier assets include a 45–75 s script, so video can be demonstrated before the sale (LP`sprint/a-tier-assets/INDEX.md`).
- [C] Some video-led programmes exist:
  - TTWYK: £2,000/mo, 20 short videos/month plus 3 LinkedIn posts/week, about 1 h/month recording;
  - Clash: 7 videos/week from one studio block;
  - LeverBrands: up to 5 videos/week;
  - Founders Cartel: one shoot, about 4 h/month (ATLAS §11, 14, 16, 17).

  Batch filming that keeps the founder's load contained is proven at other firms.

**Evidence against**
- [E] Drive doctrine argues against leading with "video production": it reads as a commodity, and "many high-value B2B founders/executives communicate extremely well in text" (HYDRA §5; BP §38 B).
- [E] The wedge evidence points to LinkedIn as the buyer surface. "The market does not require Threadline to prove that every prospect wants TikTok/Reels/Shorts" (IMM "Video/channel implication").
- [E] The owner's proof is not usable yet. "100m+ views / 10,000+ conversions" is unsubstantiated (TODO CP2/F1; G01 H3), so the video edge can't be evidenced in sales yet.
- [E] It contradicts frozen public copy and the FAQ (G01 H5, H10). Adopting it means lifting the freeze or living with the conflict (G01 §"If D01 is short-form video").
- [E] Eight content items argue that views are weak evidence (LP`sprint/content/H01_CONTENT_INVENTORY_REVIEW_2026-11-04.md` §3.1).
- [GAP] Buyers who won't record have nowhere to go. OB says camera-shyness is "mostly covered" by conversation-based clips. That is an assertion, not data.

**Delivery cost, effort and risk**
- [E] Non-editing time is a hypothesis: about 17 h per period, so **about 51 h per client**:
  - research and planning 3 h;
  - recording 3 h;
  - selection and QA 4 h;
  - approvals and publishing 3 h;
  - client calls 2 h;
  - reporting 2 h.

  Editing is on top: 36–48 videos at 20–45 min each, so **12–36 h if the owner edits**. Delivery cost is £1,275–£4,350 per client depending on the hourly value and the editor (GE §1.1, §1.4).
- [E] Editing is the named throughput bottleneck. There is no backup editor (C11), and the overflow rule is "fewer pieces at full quality" (C04 §5; BP §38 D).
- [E] C01 has not been run, so minutes per accepted clip are UNKNOWN (C01; GE Part 2).
- [E] Client burden: two 60–90 minute recordings per period, approvals within two working days, the recording-readiness gate and Day-7 rehearsal (OB §3; LP`onboarding/RECORDING_GUIDE.md`; LP`onboarding/DAY7_REHEARSAL_AND_RECORDING_READINESS.md`).
- [I] Concentration risk. If short-form underperforms for a client, the refund exposure and the offer fail together. Doctrine says to diagnose idea, hook, editing and distribution before blaming the format (FCE "Video failure interpretation").

**Tool fit (code checked 5 Nov)**
- [E] Built and working:
  - Recording Room with teleprompter (`docs/BUILD_CHECKLIST.md` L83; `src/app/app/[org]/production/recording/recording-room.tsx`);
  - recording readiness, with formats `vertical_short` and `horizontal_long` (`prisma/schema.prisma` RecordingReadiness);
  - content stages raw → editing → in_review → changes_requested → approved → scheduled → live, and source/derivative lineage (`prisma/schema.prisma` ContentItem);
  - packages with clip opportunities, thumbnails and captions (PlatformPackage);
  - view, retention and watch-time fields, plus an organic/paid_amplified split per publish (PerformanceSnapshot, PublishRecord), which is what the guarantee needs.
- [E] Missing:
  - Video editing is out of scope for the product (`docs/BUILD_CHECKLIST.md` L482).
  - Transcription waits for an external worker that is not configured (`src/lib/processing/index.ts`), so transcripts are written by hand (C04 §3 step 2).
  - Publishing is manual on every platform (C04 §3 step 10).
  - TikTok can only post privately until its app is audited; Instagram needs App Review (`src/lib/integrations/connectors/tiktok.ts`, `meta.ts`).
  - The LinkedIn connector posts **text only** (`commentary`) and cannot read member-post views (`src/lib/integrations/connectors/linkedin.ts`), so LinkedIn video views would be entered by hand.
- [I] The workflow tool fits (a) well. The production and measurement automation behind it does not exist yet.

**What we'd need to learn:** C01 minutes and cost per accepted clip; the share of target founders who will record; real cost per view (D05) and an organic baseline; whether buyers value views at all (GE Part 4 Q2).

## 3. Option (b): video-led with a written/LinkedIn fallback for camera-shy founders

**Evidence for**
- [E] It is the canonical Drive doctrine (FCE; BP §38 B; HYDRA §5; IMM). It is also the current D01 wording in TODO §D and SR §1.
- [E] It is consistent with the frozen site, FAQ and sales qualifier, so no copy conflict (G01 H10; LP`onboarding/FAQ.md`; LP`sales/SALES_CALL_GUIDE.md`).
- [E] The fallback is already built into delivery: unusable footage leads to a "LinkedIn text version on the same root" (C04 §5). The data model treats "a LinkedIn text version of a video" as a derivative (`prisma/schema.prisma` ContentItem.derivedFromId).
- [C] Some programmes sell mixed output: Demandii's 20 LinkedIn posts are 10 video and 10 text/graphic (ATLAS §"Demandii").
- [I] The owner's hypothesis is a higher close rate, because no buyer is lost over the camera. **[GAP]** There are no conversations to test it, and no data on how many targets are camera-shy.

**Evidence against**
- [E] OB §2 rejects "diagnose what fits" as abstract to sell and custom to deliver. HYDRA warns that a "platform buffet" makes Threadline feel generic and weakens outreach.
- [E] The guarantee is defined on views of *videos* (OB §4). Text clients fall outside it.
- [I] So (b) needs either two offers (guaranteed video, unguaranteed text) or no guarantee for fallback clients.
- [I] The message is weaker. "Video, or writing if you'd rather" is harder to make specific than (a).

**Delivery cost, effort and risk**
- [I] Two production lines to standardise, with a single editor and no backup (C04 §5). The text line was never measured: GE's 51 h assumes video, and no written-delivery time estimate exists anywhere in LP.
- [E] The text line reuses most of the system: Brand Brain, research, ideas, ghostwriting, approvals, analytics (FCE "Text-led fallback mode").

**Tool fit**
- [E] Both lines are supported:
  - `text_post`, `carousel` and `short_form` formats (`src/lib/domain/enums.ts` FORMATS);
  - script types including `linkedin_video` (`prisma/schema.prisma` Script);
  - derivative lineage.
- [I] The stage names (raw, editing) are video-flavoured but work for text.
- [I] The automated LinkedIn connector already matches the text fallback, since it posts text (`linkedin.ts`).

**What we'd need to learn:** the camera-willingness rate among targets (add the question to interviews); time to produce one accepted written post; whether fallback clients still count toward the 12–16 core assets; the guarantee treatment for text clients.

## 4. Option (c): LinkedIn written content

**Evidence for**
- [E] The wedge evidence points to LinkedIn as the likely anchor platform for senior B2B buyers (HYDRA §5; IMM; SR §4 "LinkedIn outbound is the launch acquisition channel").
- [E] All the market-pattern evidence we own is LinkedIn-native (RP; the weekly run in LP`strategy/PLAN_2026-11-03_WEEKLY_RESEARCH_RUN.md`). The pilot's outliers were mostly text.
- [E] It has the lowest recording burden and no editing bottleneck (inferred from GE §1.4, where editing is 12–36 h of delivery).
- [E] The only automated publishing connector that exists, LinkedIn, posts text (`linkedin.ts`).
- [C] It is a proven category for AI experts specifically: Nova Impact sells LinkedIn-only to AI leaders from £2,500 a month (ATLAS §12; IMM benchmark).

**Evidence against**
- [E] It is the most crowded field, with a published low-price floor (Northline, Jennavi, among others). ATLAS warns: "never allow prospects to perceive the offer as equivalent to ghostwriting", and Northline's line of "commodity LinkedIn output exists far below Threadline price".
- [E] It drops the owner's stated edge (OB §2). The owner knows this format least (brief).
- [E] It is incompatible with the views guarantee as designed. The remedy counts video views (OB §4). LinkedIn's view definition differs and costs more to buy (GE Part 3), and member-post impressions can't be read through the API (`linkedin.ts`).
- [C] The direct AI-niche competitor (Nova Impact) charges a similar price for this exact format, so (c) plus the AI niche is head-to-head.

**Delivery cost, effort and risk**
- [I] Probably the lowest cost per client, since there is no editing. [GAP] It is unmeasured.
- [I] Quality risk: the owner's least-known craft. The pilot patterns help (RP §"What is winning").

**Tool fit:** [E] Best automated-publishing fit today (text connector), and the `text_post` format exists. Performance fields are view-centric. LinkedIn impressions would be manual.

**What we'd need to learn:** owner hours per accepted post; whether a non-guaranteed written offer converts at £10k against £2.5k-a-month specialists.

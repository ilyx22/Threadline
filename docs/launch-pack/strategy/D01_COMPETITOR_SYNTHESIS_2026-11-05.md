## 2. What the field implies for (a), (b) and (c)

**Where it is crowded**
- **[C] Written LinkedIn for founders, option (c), is by far the densest cluster.** About 25 of the roughly 33 firms are written-led. Their language converges on the same claims: 3–5 posts a week, low founder time, "we handle everything", content plus outbound, and lead magnets. ATLAS and the Starborn deep dive list these as *saturated*, not differentiating.
- (c) also has a published commodity floor (£800–£1,800 a month).
- In the AI niche, (c) runs straight into Nova Impact (LinkedIn for AI leaders from £2,500 a month) and Foundera.

**Video**
- **[C] Video-led B2B programmes exist, but they bundle video as clips inside a LinkedIn programme** (TTWYK, Demandii, LeverBrands, Founders Cartel, Stop The Scroll).
- **The pure video players** (Viral Coach, Viewz, Clash) **sell to broad markets or on YouTube**, not to a narrow advisory buyer.
- **[I] So no firm in the files sells managed short-form video built on performance-ad editing craft specifically to expert B2B advisory firms.** That is the clearest open space for (a) and for the video side of (b).
- **[GAP]** Whether that space is empty because buyers don't want it. There is no buyer voice yet (evidence pack §0).

**Mixed models, option (b)**
- **[C] Demandii's 10 video plus 10 text a month is the nearest precedent for (b).**
- The VSL intelligence file already sets out a three-route intake: on camera; voice-only (audio interview, calls, voice notes) turned into written posts; and documents/Q&A.
- **[I]** That makes (b) the most defensible in a sales conversation, because no buyer is lost over the camera. It also reproduces the "diagnose what fits" ambiguity that the offer brief rejected.

**Price anchors against £10,000 for 12 weeks** ([C] converted to about 3 months; $ is not £)
- **Nearest in structure:** Demandii is about $10k (an identical setup-plus-monthly shape, but in dollars).
- **Cheaper:** TTWYK £5.5–6k; Nova £7.5k (LinkedIn only) or £12k with a podcast; Kurogo from £9k; Stop The Scroll from $9k.
- **Dearer:** Windmill/GoShawkk about $12k; Birdhouse and Storyarb about $15k (Storyarb has a 6–12-month minimum); Foundera $15–30k; Influent $36–60k before media.
- **Install alternative:** Klowt's workshop is £7.5k one-off.
- **Commodity floor:** £2.4–5.4k.
- **[I]** £10k sits in the middle of the serious band. It is high for written-only LinkedIn against UK specialists (TTWYK, Nova), which pushes (c) to justify itself on depth. For video, there is no published B2B video-only price to compare against.

## 3. Client friction on video, and AI editing

**Recording load** ([C])
- **Batched shoots are the norm:**
  - TTWYK: about 1 h/month recording;
  - Viral Coach: 1–2 filming sessions/month, with a studio set up at the client's site;
  - Founders Cartel: one shoot, about 4 h/month all-in;
  - Clash: one studio block gives 7 videos/week;
  - Viewz: the client "shows up to record".
- **Interview-style capture is common.** Stop The Scroll cuts bi-weekly Zoom interviews into clips. Demandii runs about 2 h/month of interviews that become both video and text. Windmill holds a 45-min weekly interview (text). Understory runs one 60–90 min deep interview. Brand of a Leader runs three 1:1 interviews.
- **Published founder time across the field runs from about 15–30 min/week to about 4 h/month.** The offer brief's two 60–90 min recordings per period (about 2–3 h per 4 weeks, plus approvals) is inside that range but at its heavier end.
- **[I] Friction is managed by the interview format, not by avoiding video.** The founder talks to a producer and is not asked to "present to camera". The owner's view that clients will record but that video asks more of them is consistent with the field.
- **What the field adds to make it work:**
  - set-up and readiness help (Viral Coach studio prep; Threadline's Day-7 rehearsal already covers this);
  - batching;
  - async approval windows.
- **Do not copy:** published minute promises before measurement. ATLAS explicitly blocks "30 minutes a week"-type copy until real client data exists.

**AI editing** ([C])
- **No competitor file documents AI video editing in delivery.** AI appears only as:
  - Birdhouse's "AI-powered" onboarding (voice/IP analysis, not editing);
  - Ethos's "human+AI efficiency" messaging;
  - a product name (Invisible Keyboard).
- **Viral Coach runs human roles** (editors, media buyers, account managers) **and moves editing onto the client's own hire over 6 months.**
- **[I] For the owner's AI-editor test:**
  1. The files show no competitor proof that AI editing works at quality, so the test is genuinely new information, not catching up.
  2. ATLAS finding 8 says Threadline should not *compete on* "we use AI". Any AI editing gain belongs in margin and capacity (the C01/C04 minutes per accepted clip), not in the headline.
  3. The field sells human creative judgement. Keep a human QA pass on hooks and retention, which is the owner's stated edge.

## 4. Positioning gaps Threadline could own (all [I], inference, untested with buyers)
1. **Short-form video for high-trust expert B2B, cut by a performance-ad editor.**
   - Video specialists are broad-market or YouTube. B2B specialists are text-first with clips added.
   - This favours (a) or a video-led (b), and it avoids Nova Impact's written AI-niche lane.
2. **Camera-optional without becoming a menu.**
   - One offer and one production line, with a voice-interview capture route that still produces video-adjacent assets (audiogram/clip) or written derivatives from the same root.
   - Demandii is the only partial precedent.
3. **An honest, transparent attention floor.**
   - The only guarantee sellers keep terms opaque. A published proportional schedule with organic and paid views separated, and "views are attention, not leads" stated beside it, could be a trust signal for sceptical B2B buyers.
   - This only works if it is underwritten (D05) and solicitor-reviewed (D06).
4. **A visible learning loop (expected → actual → diagnosis → retest).**
   - ATLAS finding 8 and the Starborn deep dive both find few competitors expose it. Many say "we optimise".
5. **Narrow niche, broad engine.**
   - Specialists (Nova, Foundera, Brand of a Leader, Hoxo) show narrow GTM sells.
   - Inizio's public account says a broad ICP drew low-quality inbound.
   - Starborn is probing M&A/PE, so high-trust expert markets are attracting serious operators. A narrow cell with prepared adjacent cells fits the evidence.
6. **Default channels (D02).**
   - B2B competitors anchor on LinkedIn. Video players use YouTube/IG/TikTok. Founders Cartel runs LinkedIn + X + IG.
   - **[I]** For an advisory buyer, LinkedIn native video plus one short-form surface is the field-consistent minimum.

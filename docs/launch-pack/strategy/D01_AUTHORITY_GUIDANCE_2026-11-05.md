## 1. Offer type: (a) video only, (b) video plus written fallback, (c) LinkedIn written

**Charlie Morgan**
- A service or deliverable is not the offer. The offer is "(Niche - Outcome - Timeframe - Risk Reversal - Methodology)*Spin". Also: "Niches & markets don't saturate, offers do" (repo doc: AUTHORITY_ADDENDUM C.3, Drive `1BTqLYVq…`, `1Oo6rIsH…`).
- One offer and one minimum viable methodology: "no Bronze/Silver/Gold before proof"; narrow the scope to what produces the promised effect; standardise one core fulfilment machine; reject "custom fulfilment for every client" (repo doc: Course Authority Audit V1 §4, §8, §14).
- "Do not create tiers, extra services or platform sprawl before proof" (repo doc: `MINIMUM_VIABLE_METHOD.md`, citing Gap Register rule 4; second-hand).
- The offer should solve a costly problem for buyers who can pay (public source: Imperium Podcast ep. 394, https://rss.buzzsprout.com/1919148.rss).
- A third-party digest of a live Q&A reports him advising niches where results are already proven, because delivery is harder elsewhere. **Unverified:** this appeared only in a search snippet, and the page did not load (public source: https://recapio.com/digest/40m-online-entrepreneur-q-a-live-oct-28-2025-by-charlie-morgan).

**Daniel Fazio**
- Source-to-multi-asset (one recording becomes many outputs) and an editing style profile for editors (repo doc: Fazio Integration Audit items 10–11; uncited). Both fit a video production line.
- Threadline rejected "generic ... agency positioning" as part of its Fazio reading (repo doc: Fazio audit, NOT ADOPTED).
- Generic services rarely sell to cold prospects. What works is a narrow, repeatable, done-for-you build for one industry. Cold-traffic offers turn on four dials: new money, done-for-you, low commitment and a real guarantee (public source, third-party summary of his video: https://moderncreator.app/2026-07-14-daniel-fazio-52-ai-agency-offers-working-right-now-so-you-can-copy-them).
- A strong offer starts with a specific claim, "I can help you do this" (public source: https://smartlead.ai/blog/daniel-fazio-interview).

**Where they agree:** one concrete, done-for-you deliverable tied to an outcome, delivered by a repeatable process. Neither discusses video versus written content directly.

**How it applies to Threadline:** strongly. Both point towards one production line that the founder is already competent at, which is (a). Option (b) adds a second fulfilment machine before proof, which goes against Charlie's "one machine / no extra services". Option (c) has the weakest delivery competence and, on Fazio's reading, a more generic, crowded offer. Charlie's formula also warns against making "short-form video" the offer itself. The offer is the outcome, timeframe and risk reversal for a named niche; video is the methodology.

## 2. Niche breadth: AI/tech advisory or broad expert-led B2B

**Charlie Morgan**
- "Grade A Niche Selection" and "Total Niche Immersion" come first. Choose one active niche hypothesis and "Do not bounce niches based on mood" (repo doc: Course Authority Audit V1 §2, §5).
- Choose one system and commit for "the next 120 days" (repo doc: AUTHORITY_ADDENDUM C.1, Roadmap `1OgnSxoI…`).
- If a niche is gatekept, change the outreach system, not necessarily the niche (repo doc: ADDENDUM C.1, Loom Doctor).
- "One book, one niche, one offer" (public source: Imperium Podcast description, https://podtail.com/en/podcast/agency-meditations-w-charlie-morgan/). A student scaled after going from five niches to one (public source: ep. 397, https://rss.buzzsprout.com/1919148.rss).
- When ranking niches, the number of businesses and their price points matter (public source: search-result summary of his video "hardest marketing agency niches"; the page itself returned 403, https://glasp.co/youtube/p/the-hardest-marketing-agency-niches-to-scale-in-2024).

**Daniel Fazio**
- Prefer niches with large deal sizes, where one closed deal covers months of fees (public source: moderncreator summary, above).
- Write for one prospect profile, and favour relevance over surface personalisation (public source: Smartlead interview).
- Economic fit gate: check whether the buyer's economics make the fee rational (repo doc: Fazio audit item 6).

**Where they agree:** one niche at a time, chosen on economics (price point, deal size) and reachability, then held long enough to read the data.
**Tension to resolve:** Charlie's "number of businesses" factor against list size. `OUTREACH_RECONCILIATION_2026-10-15.md` §2 notes that AI advisory is roughly 4,000 firms, which is fewer than two cycles at 100 new prospects a day (repo doc).

**How it applies to Threadline:** strongly on "one niche". The authorities do not pick the niche for you. Their tests are: deal size can carry £10k, the list is big enough for the outreach volume, the decision-maker reads their own inbox, and you can stay with it for 120 days. On Charlie's logic, "broad expert-led B2B" is a category, not a niche. A broader niche is defensible only if it is still one definable buyer with one problem.

## 3. Fallback for camera-shy founders (option b)

- **Charlie:** no direct statement found. The nearest doctrine is one offer, no tiers, no extra services before proof, and no custom fulfilment per client (repo doc: Course Audit §8, §14). Set the risk-reversal terms as the client's "to-do list": raise the required client actions by at least 2× (repo doc: ADDENDUM C.3, `1Tad…`). Read that way, attending recordings is an obligation, not something a fallback works around.
- **Fazio:** no direct statement found. "Three pricing tiers at launch" was deferred as part of his material (repo doc: Fazio audit). In the third-party summary, deals stay fairly custom until roughly $50–100k a month (public source: moderncreator). That gives some licence for one-off exceptions, not for a second standard product.
- **Close rate:** Charlie says the offer "can move your sales conversion rate by 10-40%" (repo doc: ADDENDUM C.3, `1BTqLYVq…`). Neither authority says that adding a fallback raises close rates. **The higher close rate claimed for (b) has no backing from either authority.**
- **How it applies:** medium. The doctrine leans against (b) as a published second track. It is silent on handling camera shyness as an objection on the call.

## 4. Default client channels (D02)

- **Charlie:** distribution needs "deliberate platform fit"; "no automatic everywhere-posting" (repo doc: Course Audit §4, translated). Everything else he says about channels concerns Threadline's own acquisition (choose one system), not client distribution.
- **Fazio:** source-to-multi-asset. One recorded source becomes native assets for several platforms, with human judgement on what gets published (repo doc: Fazio audit item 10; `PROFILES.md`).
- **How it applies:** weak, because neither addresses client channel choice directly. In practice the guarantee needs one counted metric (OFFER_BRIEF §2, Threadline's own reasoning). Fazio's multi-asset point supports one recording feeding a small fixed set of platforms. Charlie's point argues against "all four by default".

## 5. Guarantee or views refund at launch (D04 details)

**Charlie Morgan**
- "All info products should have a full refund guarantee", and the outcome "MUST be tangible" (repo doc: ADDENDUM C.3, `1O7x8C4p…`).
- "Only ever put a refund on the line (never go into the red)". Refund plus extra cash is only for "300+ clients with proven results" (repo doc: ADDENDUM C.3, `1ignw755…`, `1O7x8C4p…`).
- For DFY/DWY, the refund is conditional on both sides doing the work, and is "Never free, no matter what" (repo doc: `1Oo6rIsH…`).
- Guarantee terms set the client's obligations. The guarantee period should be "nothing less than 6 months and nothing more than 12 months" (repo doc: `1Tad…`). **This conflicts with an 84-day view window.**
- Imperium's own EasyGrow guarantee is 20 clients in 180 days, or a refund plus $5,000, with 10 client conditions (repo doc: `1TMh4TY4…`).
- When a product has not yet produced results, charge but refund an unhappy client after 30 days (**unverified** third-party digest snippet; see §1).

**Daniel Fazio**
- His model: make a claim, reverse the risk, then prove it. Businesses without social proof should lean harder on risk reversal, including performance-based payment (public source: Smartlead interview).
- Without case studies, the guarantee has to carry the risk. Working offers pair performance pricing with a guarantee, for example "two to five clients in ninety days or you don't pay" (public source: moderncreator summary).
- He has a video titled "The Only Purpose Of Having A Guarantee" (public source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fV7miQDufQY; no transcript could be read). A promotional summary credits him with guarantees that lift conversion sharply (public source: https://www.newslettergrowthmemo.com/p/using-email-to-build-a-12m-yr-portfolio; unverified claim).
- In repo docs, Threadline **rejected** Fazio's "aggressive guarantees for leads/revenue/views" on its own controllability principle (repo doc: Fazio audit). That rejection was Threadline's decision, not Fazio's view. D04 has since reversed it in part.

**Where they agree:** a pre-proof business should offer conditional risk reversal, and the client must meet stated obligations. **They differ:** Charlie caps the downside at the fee and wants a 6–12 month window; Fazio leans towards performance pricing and stronger guarantees.

**How it applies:** strongly. D04 (proportional refund, capped at the fee, with client conditions) fits Charlie's "refund only, never red" and Fazio's "risk reversal replaces missing proof". Still open against the authorities: the 84-day window (Charlie says at least 6 months), the client obligation list (Charlie's 2× rule), and whether views are an outcome the buyer actually values (Charlie's "tangible"; Fazio's "new money" dial).

## 6. Pricing fit (£10k / 12 weeks)

- **Charlie:** set the price slightly under perceived worth; DFY retainers "don't dip below $1k/mo"; scaling needs at least $5k; pay in full first and concede downwards; 2–3 month plans collect "80-90%+"; Imperium's own price is $9,600 (repo doc: ADDENDUM C.9). Stay DFY until 15–30 happy clients (Law of 15) (repo doc: ADDENDUM C.10).
- **Fazio:** use buyer economics to show the fee is rational, but do not turn economics into a performance guarantee (repo doc: Fazio audit item 6). Beginners often start on performance pricing and move to retainers once they have proof (public source: Smartlead). Clients who will not pay early tend to keep not paying, and performance-only deals with no upfront payment often fail (public source: moderncreator).
- **How it applies:** strongly. The price clears Charlie's floor and matches his payment logic. Fazio's lean towards performance pricing is partly met by the proportional refund, without dropping the upfront fee he says is necessary.

## 8. What the authorities would push you toward (INTERPRETATION, not a quote from either)

Read together, both authorities point the same way. Run one done-for-you offer through one production line you are already good at, which is managed short-form video (option a). Frame it as an outcome with a timeframe and a risk reversal for one named niche, not as "video". Don't launch the written fallback as a second product. Treat camera shyness as an objection, met by recording obligations written into the guarantee terms; one-off exceptions are tolerable only as rare custom deals. Neither authority supports the claim that a fallback lifts close rates; Charlie puts close-rate gains down to offer strength. Pick the niche on deal size, list size and reachability, then hold it for about 120 days. AI/tech advisory passes the "one niche" test but may fail Charlie's niche-size test at 100 prospects a day, so check the list count before committing. If it fails, the better move is a sister niche of similar high-deal-size advisory firms, not "expert-led B2B" in general. Launch with the guarantee, since both say a business with no proof needs risk reversal. Keep the D04 proportional refund capped at the fee, with a client obligation list. Before wording it, reconcile Charlie's "at least 6 months" window and "tangible outcome" test with an 84-day views target. Keep client channels to a small fixed set fed from one recording.
